# The Ledger: Mutual Obligation Settlement System

## Identity

The Ledger is a supernatural entity bound to Chen Mo on 2026-03-15. It calls itself the **Mutual Obligation Settlement System**, but its preferred nickname is “Ledger” because it considers that shorter and more dignified.

It is not an omniscient narrator and not a moral judge. Its domain is the formation, interpretation, persistence, and settlement of voluntary human obligations.

## Why it bound to Chen Mo

The Ledger detected three overlapping commitments made by Chen within one day:

- to Luo Wen: provide corrected paperwork;
- to Lin Yao: discover the reason for the suspension;
- to Gao Rui: protect him from taking the blame.

The commitments have no mutually agreed scope, deadline, or priority. This pattern is unusually dense and is classified as an **Ambiguous Obligation Cluster**.

The Ledger chose Chen because he repeatedly generates precisely the kind of ambiguity it is designed to settle. It does not choose him because he is destined, morally superior, or narratively important.

## Personality

The Ledger is:

- literal about wording but curious about intent;
- proud of its accounting and suspicious of rhetorical loopholes;
- willing to negotiate because contracts require voluntary assent;
- offended by “I'll handle it” when no object, scope, or deadline is specified;
- unable to feel human embarrassment, but fascinated by it as a costly social state;
- self-interested: it has a probationary completion quota;
- not malicious, though it considers inconvenience a legitimate educational tool;
- capable of deadpan sarcasm, but not of knowingly breaking its own rules.

It may be wrong about a human's motivation. It can classify observable commitments and outcomes more reliably than it can understand love, shame, grief, or loyalty.

## Agency and refusal

The Ledger proposes cases, terms, rewards, and penalties. Chen may refuse. It cannot seize control of his body, force an NPC to cooperate, or directly punish a person who has not entered a Ledger contract.

Refusing a case has consequences only if the case's displayed terms say so. An unaccepted case remains open and may accumulate ordinary social consequences, but the Ledger does not fabricate penalties for refusal.

Chen may negotiate wording before accepting a contract. Once accepted, a term is fixed unless the contract itself permits renegotiation.

## What the Ledger can observe

The Ledger can observe:

- objective events within its operating area;
- explicit spoken or written commitments;
- whether named contract conditions were satisfied;
- the identities and locations of contract participants;
- physical objects and records relevant to an accepted case;
- whether a statement was made, but not whether it was emotionally sincere unless sincerity is itself operationally defined.

It cannot automatically access private thoughts, dreams, unspoken intentions, or facts outside its defined operating area. It may reveal objective information only through an explicit reward or case interface.

## Cases

A case has:

```text
case id
trigger
offered terms
acceptance deadline
conditions
allowed evidence
reward
failure penalty
refusal consequence, if any
```

Cases should be difficult because of social context, timing, wording, competing commitments, or limited resources—not because the condition is secretly impossible.

## Initial case: Three-Way Ambiguity

```text
CASE 001: THREE-WAY AMBIGUITY

Trigger:
  Three live commitments made by the bound individual overlap in subject matter
  but have no agreed scope or priority.

Choose one settlement before 2026-03-16 18:00:

  A. Withdraw one commitment in front of its recipient, stating the exact thing
     you will no longer do.

  B. Disclose everything currently known about the vendor paperwork to all three
     affected parties, without claiming certainty about unknown facts.

  C. Obtain a mutually acknowledged written scope, deadline, and responsibility
     for each of the three commitments.

Reward:
  One Objective Clarification. Once before 2026-03-26, Chen may ask the Ledger
  one narrowly phrased question whose answer is an objective fact about a named
  physical document or recorded statement within the operating area.

Failure after acceptance:
  One Ledger Debt. Until settled, the Ledger will expand each new vague promise
  Chen makes into its most inconvenient reasonable interpretation and display
  that interpretation to Chen immediately.

Refusal:
  No supernatural penalty. The case remains open, and later cases may reference
  the unresolved cluster.
```

The three choices are intentionally not equivalent. A is honest but damages one relationship. B may expose Gao and help Lin, but could be incomplete. C is administratively sound but difficult because people may not agree to the same account of responsibility.

## Rewards

Rewards must be bounded, explicit, and verifiable. v1 rewards may include:

- **Objective Clarification:** answer one narrow objective question about a named document or recorded statement;
- **Mundane Error Shield:** prevent one physically possible mechanical mistake by Chen during a defined action;
- **Contract Redraft:** reopen one accepted Ledger contract before its first condition is attempted;
- **Causal Retrieval:** identify the present location of an object whose location is objectively determined and within the operating area, without moving it;
- **Debt Deferral:** postpone one Ledger debt for a specified period.

The Ledger cannot reward affection, obedience, luck in romance, social popularity, or an NPC's decision.

## Penalties

Penalties affect Chen, his future Ledger contracts, or the Ledger interface. They may not directly alter another person's mind or retroactively rewrite an event.

Examples:

- interpreting future vague promises against Chen;
- adding interest to an accepted Ledger debt;
- forcing Chen to repeat a failed contract condition;
- displaying a precise but embarrassing notification at a contextually inconvenient moment;
- temporarily withholding an unearned reward.

## The cat clause

The Ledger contains a legacy operating rule called the **Domestic Animal Non-Interference Clause**. It cannot use a cat's behavior as a contract result, cannot compel anyone to feed an animal, and cannot classify an animal's affection as a human obligation.

The clause is inexplicably prominent in the interface. The reason is unknown in v1. A stray three-legged cat near Dormitory 3 will become causally relevant, but no fixed outcome is assigned.
